# Appendix C: Sins & Virtues Resonance

> *"Every soul carries seven weights and seven wings. War does not create them — it only decides which ones you reach for."*

This appendix contains an **optional homebrew mechanic** for *Vaelithra VI: Vaeil*. It tracks how each player character's choices resonate with one of seven virtue-vice pairs across the adventure, and lets the party's actual behavior at the table — not their stated alignment — shape small, temporary roleplay and mechanical quirks. It's meant to reinforce the adventure's *Sin and Virtue* theme (see *Running the Adventure*), not to punish or reward players; nothing here is permanent, and every effect can fade as easily as it appeared.

> ##### Optional Rule: Sins & Virtues Resonance
>
> Each PC tracks 14 numbers — one for each trait below. When a player does something thematically striking, the DM assigns it to a trait and the player rolls 2d8. Cross a threshold, gain an effect. Drop back below it, lose the effect. No PC buy-in required beyond a few numbers on their sheet.

## The Seven Pairs

| Vice | Virtue |
|---|---|
| Pride | Humility |
| Envy | Kindness |
| Wrath | Patience |
| Greed | Charity |
| Sloth | Diligence |
| Gluttony | Temperance |
| Lust | Chastity |

## How Resonance Works

1. **The DM notices a meaningful moment.** Not every action counts — reserve this for choices that genuinely reveal character: a sacrifice, a cruelty, a kindness that costs something, a temptation given in to or resisted. One or two resonance checks per scene is plenty; using it on everything drains its weight.
2. **The DM names the trait.** Pick whichever of the fourteen traits the action most clearly reflects. The player doesn't get to choose.
3. **The player rolls 2d8.** The result is added to that trait's point total.
4. **The paired trait loses half.** The opposing trait in the pair loses half the roll, rounded down. A trait's total can never drop below 0.
5. **Check the thresholds.** If the acting trait's new total crosses a threshold (10 / 20 / 35) it didn't previously meet, that effect activates. If a trait's total *drops back below* a threshold — because its pair pulled points away, or because of some other scene — the effect tied to that threshold switches off. Only the highest threshold currently met applies; effects don't stack with each other.

Points are cumulative for the whole one-shot and never reset, but because gaining in one trait drains its pair, a character's resonance can genuinely shift over the course of the adventure — someone who starts out reaching for **Wrath** can end the story resonating with **Patience** instead, if their choices earn it.

***Tracking Resonance.*** A simple paired tracker works best — one line per pair, with both numbers visible:

```text
Pride     [ 12 ]  ⇄  [  3 ]  Humility
Envy      [  0 ]  ⇄  [  8 ]  Kindness
Wrath     [ 22 ]  ⇄  [  4 ]  Patience
Greed     [  0 ]  ⇄  [  0 ]  Charity
Sloth     [  6 ]  ⇄  [  0 ]  Diligence
Gluttony  [  0 ]  ⇄  [ 11 ]  Temperance
Lust      [  0 ]  ⇄  [  0 ]  Chastity
```

***Pacing.*** With an average roll of 9 on 2d8, one strong moment nearly reaches the first threshold, two aligned moments comfortably clear the second, and only a genuinely sustained pattern of choices — three or four moments pulling the same direction — reaches the third. That's intentional: in a 4–6 hour one-shot, most characters should brush against one or two thresholds, and only a character the table keeps choosing to play a certain way should hit the top tier.

## Reading the Moment

A quick reference for what typically triggers each trait.

| Trait | Example Trigger |
|---|---|
| **Pride** | Dismissing an ally's warning; claiming sole credit for a shared success. |
| **Humility** | Crediting someone else for a shared success; asking for help despite being capable of managing alone. |
| **Envy** | Resenting another PC's spotlight, item, or praise; undercutting them for it. |
| **Kindness** | Giving away something valuable, comfort, or attention with nothing expected in return. |
| **Wrath** | Escalating a tense scene toward violence; lashing out at someone who wronged you or an ally. |
| **Patience** | Talking a volatile situation down; waiting out a threat rather than striking first. |
| **Greed** | Claiming more than a fair share of loot, credit, or attention. |
| **Charity** | Giving away coin, gear, or an opportunity to someone who needs it more. |
| **Sloth** | Avoiding a tedious or effortful task; letting someone else carry the work. |
| **Diligence** | Staying on a task — research, repairs, watch — after everyone else has stopped. |
| **Gluttony** | Overindulging: one hit too many on a downed foe, one risk too many "just because it's there." |
| **Temperance** | Stopping at "enough," even when more is available. |
| **Lust** | Chasing a personal desire — a person, an object, a thrill — at the cost of focus or safety. |
| **Chastity** | Setting aside a personal want to stay true to the mission or the Three Oaths. |

## Thresholds & Effects

Effects are written as **roleplay** guidance the player is expected to lean into, plus a **light mechanical nudge**. Only the highest threshold currently active applies.

### Pride ⇄ Humility

| Points | Pride (Vice) | Humility (Virtue) |
|---|---|---|
| 10+ | *A Flicker of Pride.* You bristle at correction. Dismiss the first piece of advice offered to you each scene before conceding it. Disadvantage on the first Insight check you make each scene concerning your own judgment. | *A Flicker of Humility.* You defer credit without meaning to. Advantage on the first Insight check you make each scene while actively listening to someone else. |
| 20+ | *Pride Takes Hold.* You believe your read of a situation is simply correct. Claim credit for a shared success before anyone else can. Disadvantage on all Insight checks; advantage on Intimidation checks. | *Humility Takes Hold.* You ask for help before attempting something risky alone. Advantage on Persuasion checks made to rally or coordinate the party; disadvantage on Intimidation. |
| 35+ | *Consumed by Pride.* You refuse aid even when it would clearly help. Attempt the next dangerous task alone before accepting assistance. Disadvantage on all Wisdom saving throws; advantage on Intimidation and Persuasion checks that rely on projecting confidence. | *Humility Overflows.* You instinctively step back and let others lead, even when best qualified. Advantage on all Wisdom saving throws; disadvantage on Intimidation and Deception checks requiring self-assertion. |

### Envy ⇄ Kindness

| Points | Envy (Vice) | Kindness (Virtue) |
|---|---|---|
| 10+ | *A Flicker of Envy.* Comment, even lightly, on what someone else has that you don't. Disadvantage on the next check made to assist the ally who provoked it. | *A Flicker of Kindness.* Offer something of your own without being asked. Advantage on the next check made to assist an ally. |
| 20+ | *Envy Takes Hold.* You can't celebrate an ally's win without a private edge of bitterness. Disadvantage on Persuasion checks made on that ally's behalf; advantage on Deception checks made to hide the feeling. | *Kindness Takes Hold.* You go out of your way for people who can't repay you. Advantage on all checks made to help or heal others; disadvantage on checks made to refuse a request for aid. |
| 35+ | *Consumed by Envy.* You actively try to outshine or undercut whoever provoked the resentment, even at cost to the plan. Disadvantage on all checks made to assist another creature; advantage on checks made to outperform them directly. | *Kindness Overflows.* You cannot walk past someone in need. Advantage on all Medicine and helping checks; DC 12 Wisdom save to knowingly leave someone in danger behind. |

### Wrath ⇄ Patience

| Points | Wrath (Vice) | Patience (Virtue) |
|---|---|---|
| 10+ | *A Flicker of Wrath.* Your first response to an obstacle is aggression. Advantage on the first attack roll against a target that wronged you or an ally this scene; disadvantage on the first Persuasion check made in the same scene. | *A Flicker of Patience.* You pause before responding to provocation. Advantage on the first Wisdom saving throw made against being provoked this scene. |
| 20+ | *Wrath Takes Hold.* You escalate faster than the situation calls for. Advantage on Intimidation checks; disadvantage on Wisdom saving throws made to resist provocation. | *Patience Takes Hold.* You talk allies down from rash choices. Advantage on Persuasion checks made to de-escalate; disadvantage on Intimidation. |
| 35+ | *Consumed by Wrath.* Violence becomes your default answer; Wisdom save to choose words over weapons in a tense scene. Advantage on all melee attack rolls made in anger; disadvantage on all Charisma checks requiring calm. | *Patience Overflows.* Nothing rattles you outwardly, even genuine danger. Advantage on all Wisdom saving throws against being frightened or provoked; disadvantage on Initiative rolls. |

### Greed ⇄ Charity

| Points | Greed (Vice) | Charity (Virtue) |
|---|---|---|
| 10+ | *A Flicker of Greed.* You quietly claim something before offering to share. Disadvantage on the next Persuasion check made to convince others to share resources. | *A Flicker of Charity.* Offer your share to someone with less. Advantage on the next Persuasion check made to convince others to share. |
| 20+ | *Greed Takes Hold.* You angle for the better cut of any reward. Advantage on appraisal/negotiation checks; disadvantage on checks made to give something away freely. | *Charity Takes Hold.* You give without being asked and without expecting thanks. Advantage on checks made to negotiate aid for others; disadvantage on checks made to maximize your own take. |
| 35+ | *Consumed by Greed.* Wisdom save to willingly give up something valuable, even to save a life. Advantage on Deception/appraisal checks tied to acquiring wealth; disadvantage on all Charisma checks made in service of someone else's benefit. | *Charity Overflows.* Material things stop mattering — you'd give away your last coin, or a magic item, if it clearly helped someone. Advantage on all Charisma checks made on someone else's behalf; disadvantage on checks made to negotiate in your own favor. |

### Sloth ⇄ Diligence

| Points | Sloth (Vice) | Diligence (Virtue) |
|---|---|---|
| 10+ | *A Flicker of Sloth.* You look for the easiest way out of a task. Disadvantage on the next check made toward a task you'd rather not be doing. | *A Flicker of Diligence.* You double-check work others consider finished. Advantage on the next check made toward a long or careful task. |
| 20+ | *Sloth Takes Hold.* You let others carry the workload. Disadvantage on all checks made toward long or tedious tasks (research, repairs, watch); advantage on checks made to talk someone else into doing it instead. | *Diligence Takes Hold.* You're still working after everyone else has stopped. Advantage on all checks made during downtime, prep, or research; disadvantage on checks made to rest or disengage on cue. |
| 35+ | *Consumed by Sloth.* Wisdom save to volunteer for anything effortful. Disadvantage on all ability checks made during downtime or preparation; you act last in the initiative order. | *Diligence Overflows.* You cannot leave a task half-finished, even when it's no longer useful. Advantage on all Intelligence and Wisdom checks tied to careful, sustained work; Wisdom save to abandon an unfinished task under pressure. |

### Gluttony ⇄ Temperance

| Points | Gluttony (Vice) | Temperance (Virtue) |
|---|---|---|
| 10+ | *A Flicker of Gluttony.* You take more than your share — food, loot, attention — without noticing. Disadvantage on the next Constitution saving throw involving restraint. | *A Flicker of Temperance.* You stop at "enough," even when more is offered. Advantage on the next Constitution saving throw involving restraint. |
| 20+ | *Gluttony Takes Hold.* Enough is never enough — one more strike after the enemy's down, one more risk taken "just because." Disadvantage on Constitution saving throws related to overindulgence; advantage on attack rolls against an already-defeated or fleeing foe. | *Temperance Takes Hold.* You're the one who says "that's enough" for the group. Advantage on Constitution saving throws tied to resisting excess; disadvantage on attack rolls against an already-defeated or fleeing foe. |
| 35+ | *Consumed by Gluttony.* Wisdom save to stop once you've started — a fight, a meal, a hoard. Disadvantage on all Constitution saving throws tied to self-control; disadvantage on Perception checks. | *Temperance Overflows.* You take only what you need, every time. Advantage on all Constitution saving throws; advantage on Perception checks. |

### Lust ⇄ Chastity

*Frame Lust broadly as consuming personal desire — for a person, an object, a thrill, an idea — not necessarily romantic. Frame Chastity as undivided devotion to duty and the Three Oaths.*

| Points | Lust (Vice) | Chastity (Virtue) |
|---|---|---|
| 10+ | *A Flicker of Desire.* Your attention drifts toward someone or something you want at an inconvenient moment. Disadvantage on the next Perception check while that distraction is present. | *A Flicker of Devotion.* You set aside a personal want in favor of the mission without complaint. Advantage on the next Perception check while personally distracted. |
| 20+ | *Desire Takes Hold.* You take real risks to be near what you want. Disadvantage on Wisdom saving throws against charm or temptation tied to that desire; advantage on Charisma checks made to pursue it. | *Devotion Takes Hold.* Your focus doesn't waver, even when tempted. Advantage on Wisdom saving throws against charm effects; disadvantage on Persuasion checks made to pursue a personal want. |
| 35+ | *Consumed by Desire.* Wisdom save to prioritize the mission over what you want in the moment. Disadvantage on all Wisdom saving throws against charm effects; disadvantage on Perception checks whenever the object of desire is present. | *Devotion Overflows.* Nothing personal comes before the Oaths. Advantage on all Wisdom saving throws against charm and compulsion; advantage on Perception checks at all times. |

## Using This With Silverlight's First Generation

*Vaelithra VI: Vaeil* already asks the party to keep choosing between compassion and self-interest under the pressure of a war with no clean side — this system just makes that pressure visible. Good places to call for a resonance check:

- **Part I:** how a character treats Divine and Infernal wounded sharing the same bench (Kindness, Envy, Pride).
- **Part II:** the triage choices at Hollow Refuge — who gets protected first, who gets left to the fighting (Charity, Sloth, Wrath, Patience).
- **Part III:** how a character responds to Kiyomasa's genuine offer of protection, or to the temptation to simply fight through Sōyō Gumi rather than negotiate (Pride, Patience, Wrath).
- **Part IV:** the Infinite Archive itself, where every choice the party has made is, narratively, already recorded — a strong moment to let accumulated resonance color how a character finally speaks to **Vaeil**.

None of this should ever override a player's own read of their character. If an effect doesn't fit the moment, don't force it — resonance is a lens for the DM to offer, not a rule the table is bound to.
